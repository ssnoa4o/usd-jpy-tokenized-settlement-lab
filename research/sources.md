# Sources

Access dates: rows first read on 2026-11-01 keep that date. PDFs were read on 2026-11-02 (see "Local PDF copies"). Project: independent research prototype. No listed publisher has reviewed, approved, or endorsed this project.

Verification caveat:
- HTML pages (read 2026-11-01) were read through a fetch tool that returns a model-made summary, not raw text. Wording must be re-checked in a browser before any verbatim quote. "Verified (HTML summary)" means the page was opened and its claims were noted from that summary.
- Seven PDFs were read on 2026-11-02 from page-by-page extracted text (one text file per page), not from the visual layout. Tables, charts and figures may be incomplete or garbled in the extraction (for example BIS-2 figures, CLS-4 Figures 1 and 3, BOJ-8 charts). Two-column pages (BOJ-8, CLS-4) can interleave lines. Numbers read from a chart are not used.
- Page numbers are written as `PDF p.N / printed p.M`. "PDF p." is the page index in the file. "Printed p." is the number printed on the page. The offset for each document is in "Local PDF copies".
- A quote from a PDF appears only where it matches the extracted text exactly. Quotes from HTML pages come from the model-made summary, so they are not confirmed wording and must be checked against the original page, or paraphrased, before publication of the final report. Everything else is a paraphrase labelled with the publisher ("BIS says", "BOJ says", "CLS says").
- Figures in sources are the publishers' own statements. They are never inputs to, or validation targets for, our experiments.

Status key: verified = page opened and used for the stated claims; partially verified = only part was read (the pages read are listed); UNVERIFIED = not opened, not found, or content not readable. UNVERIFIED items must not be cited for facts.

## Notes on BIS-2-PDF (read 2026-11-02; BIS's statements, not ours)

Printed page = PDF page - 5. All items below are what the BIS report says.

1. Scope and status. BIS says the prototype is "an experimental implementation" and "not designed to meet all operational, security, resilience, governance or assurance requirements expected of live financial market infrastructures" (Glossary, PDF p.96 / printed p.91). The report says its views "do not necessarily represent those of the participating institutions" (PDF p.5). Foreign exchange integration is listed as out of scope for the prototype (PDF p.15 / printed p.10, "Calibrated scope"). Liquidity saving mechanisms are not prioritised (PDF p.15; PDF p.21 / printed p.16; PDF p.90 / printed p.85).
2. Failure handling and rollback (prototype workflow). BIS says settlement proceeds only once all required balances are locked; if any participant cannot commit the required balance within the agreed window, the payment does not execute, locked funds are unlocked and the payment is aborted (PDF p.23 / printed p.18). Stages have explicit timeouts enforced by a payment coordinator; a timeout or a negative outcome terminates the workflow and is recorded as an on-ledger event (PDF p.48 / printed p.43). A failed validation terminates the payment before any assets are locked (PDF p.52 / printed p.47); a "not ready" or timeout terminates it before locking (PDF p.53 / printed p.48). Locks are either spent (commit, `spendLock()`) or released (cancel, `cancelLock()`) according to the coordinator outcome (PDF p.55 / printed p.50; PDF p.56 / printed p.51). Testing was scenario-based and qualitative, and included "Timeout handling and workflow termination at different stages, including clean abort and release of locked balances"; it did not include performance benchmarking, stress testing, security assurance or operational resilience assessment (PDF p.59 / printed p.54). The report's "Exception handling: Procedures for errors, reversals and other exceptional events" is listed as future rulebook work (PDF p.92 / printed p.87).
3. Atomicity is defined at workflow level. BIS says updates to the unifying ledger may occur before, and asynchronously from, updates to the jurisdictional ledgers, all following an "atomic settlement trigger" (PDF p.70 / printed p.65). The glossary says atomicity is used in the workflow sense, "not as a statement about legal settlement finality or synchronous multi-ledger transactions" (PDF p.93 / printed p.88).
4. PvP. BIS says its PvP reuses the locking and settlement constructs, uses a single PvP coordinator, and both legs commit together or both cancel together (PDF pp.56-57 / printed pp.51-52). BIS says the prototype "does not implement the full range of production-grade PvP features such as netting, batch PvP coordination, liquidity reservation windows, or integration with external trading venues and risk systems" (PDF p.57 / printed p.52). Footnote 15 says confirmation of payee, path discovery and compliance checks are not implemented for PvP (PDF p.21 / printed p.16).
5. USD/JPY PvP: NOT FOUND. Searched all 97 pages for USD, JPY, yen, Japan, dollar. Japan appears as a participating jurisdiction and Bank of Japan participant (PDF p.13 / printed p.8; PDF p.96 / printed p.91), in data-localisation text (PDF p.77), and in three illustrative payment journeys (PDF pp.81-87 / printed pp.76-82). Journey 3 is a GBP/JPY liquidity-balancing example prepared as a PvP instruction (PDF p.85-87 / printed pp.80-82). BIS says these journeys illustrate how "a Project Agorá-like platform could reshape" payment execution (PDF p.81 / printed p.76); they are not test results. No USD/JPY pair is named as a tested PvP.
6. Quantified liquidity or settlement-risk findings: NOT FOUND. Searched for percentages, "per cent", seconds, minutes, basis points, trillion, billion, million. The only numbers hit were market-context figures (cross-border payments of USD 195 trillion in 2024, PDF p.12 / printed p.7; wholesale share 91% in 2023, PDF p.14 / printed p.9) and the qualitative phrase "settlement occurring in seconds once funds or liquidity are locked" (PDF p.8 / printed p.3). BIS says the business requirements "are not intended as measured operational outcomes" (PDF p.19 / printed p.14) and that "benefits will only be quantifiable when applied to more realistic conditions" (PDF p.81 / printed p.76). The report assessment is a red-amber-green qualitative mapping (PDF pp.80-81 / printed pp.75-76). On liquidity, BIS says atomic settlement was pursued as a benefit "eliminating settlement risk and reducing liquidity needs" (PDF p.15 / printed p.10; a stated aim, not a measured result); future work should address "potential liquidity demands arising from gross settlement, particularly as volumes scale" (PDF p.91 / printed p.86).
7. Real-value testing figures from BIS-1 (CHF 800,000, 30 transactions, 17 scenarios, about 80 seconds, CHF-GBP and GBP-USD PvP): NOT FOUND in the report. Searched: real-value, real value, CHF, 800,000, 80 seconds, 17 scenarios, 30 transactions, July. The report describes user acceptance testing that was "scenario-based and qualitative" (PDF p.59 / printed p.54).
8. "Tokenised correspondent banking": NOT FOUND as a phrase (searched case-insensitively for "tokenised correspondent" and "tokenized correspondent" in all seven PDFs). BIS wording is "preserves correspondent banking as the backbone of global payments" (PDF p.6 / printed p.1) and "implementing the correspondent banking model on a programmable platform" (PDF p.10 / printed p.5, two lines).
9. Settlement finality. BIS says the legal analysis "highlighted settlement finality is achievable across all seven participating jurisdictions" and that further work is needed, such as defining technical, operational and contractual requirements (PDF p.7 / printed p.2). In Chapter 5 BIS describes a model in which the atomic settlement trigger would be given legal significance by rulebooks, and says the operating model and its legal characterisation "were out of scope" (PDF pp.70-71 / printed pp.65-66). BIS adds that settlement finality "may warrant further elaboration in the individual participating jurisdictions" (PDF p.78 / printed p.73) and that differences in legal effects across jurisdictions "could create operational risks" (PDF p.88 / printed p.83). The legal analysis "does not make definitive legal determinations" (PDF p.61 / printed p.56). The phrase "under review" (BIS-1) was not found.
10. Participants. BIS says seven central banks and more than 40 regulated financial institutions (PDF p.6 / printed p.1; PDF p.10 / printed p.5). The seven are named (PDF pp.13-14 / printed pp.8-9). The Bank of Canada is not mentioned in the report (searched "Canada"; BOC-1 says it joined on the report date).
11. Terminology. BIS defines settlement risk in Table 1 as encompassing "both credit and liquidity risk" (PDF p.17 / printed p.12), and also says atomic settlement eliminates "credit and settlement risk" (PDF p.8 / printed p.3) and "eliminating settlement risk" (PDF p.7 / printed p.2). BCBS-1 says PVP arrangements will not eliminate replacement cost risk or liquidity risk (PDF p.17 of BCBS-1). The two publishers use "settlement risk" differently. Do not repeat either claim without stating whose definition is used.

## Open source issues

1. BOJ-6 date inconsistency (2026-05-29 vs. URL suggesting 2026-06-11): resolve in a browser. Unchanged.
2. BIS-1 header date is stale relative to its content. Unchanged.
3. Participant counts: 7 vs. 8 central banks is explained by the Bank of Canada joining (BOC-1). The BIS-2 PDF names seven and does not mention Canada. The 22 vs. 28 institutions in RVT on BIS-1 is still not reconciled; the PDF gives "more than 40 regulated financial institutions" and no RVT counts.
4. Wording "tokenised correspondent banking" was NOT found on any official page or in any of the seven PDFs. Do not use it. BIS-2 uses "implementing the correspondent banking model on a programmable platform" (PDF p.10 / printed p.5).
5. BOJ sources differ on whether the sandbox is internal (BOJ-5) or within the CBDC Forum (BOJ-6). We do not say which. Unchanged; the BOJ-7 Chapter VII pages do not settle it.
6. Settlement finality wording. BIS-1 (HTML summary) says finality is "under review". BIS-3 (HTML summary) says it is achievable in all seven jurisdictions with requirements still to be defined. The BIS-2 report says the second (executive summary, PDF p.7 / printed p.2) and also says an operating model and its legal characterisation in each jurisdiction were out of scope (PDF pp.70-71), that some points may warrant further elaboration (PDF p.78), and that differences in legal effects could create operational risks (PDF p.88). "Under review" was not found in the report. The two web pages may describe the same status differently; we do not call them contradictory, and BIS-1 wording remains unchecked.
7. BIS-1 real-value testing claims (CHF 800,000, 30 transactions, 17 scenarios, about 80 seconds, CHF-GBP and GBP-USD PvP) are absent from the BIS-2 PDF. BIS-1 dates that testing to July 2026, after the report's landing-page date of 2026-05-27, so their absence is expected and neither confirms nor contradicts them. They stay single-source. The report describes qualitative user acceptance testing instead (PDF p.59 / printed p.54).
8. BCBS-1 page numbering: the annex starts at printed p.27 (PDF p.33). The earlier note "p.33" is the PDF page index. The glossary starts at printed p.32 (PDF p.38).
9. FXYCS operator: BOJ-8 (2007) says the Tokyo Bankers Association; BOJ-10 (2025) says the Japanese Bankers Association. Use the name with its year. The "Oct 2008" month from a search summary is not in either PDF (BOJ-10 says "2008").
